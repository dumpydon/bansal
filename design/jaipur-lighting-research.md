# Jaipur after sundown — research and art direction

Research date: 23 October 2026. Scope: the Bansal Sales Corporation website’s existing illustrated Jaipur, with day, evening and night appearances. This is an original artistic interpretation, not a photographic reconstruction or a statement of present municipal operating hours.

## What the evidence supports

Jaipur’s illuminated heritage streetscape is an everyday architectural-lighting idea, not merely a Diwali treatment. The city’s permanent illumination programme and the intended character of its light are independently documented:

- **The lighting designer’s own account.** Lucent Worldwide’s September 2017 project announcement describes the first 2.7 km phase including Hawa Mahal, with an emphasis on architectural beauty, limited glare and controlled light pollution. That supports restraint rather than a blanket luminous outline. [Lucent Worldwide: Jaipur Walled City Lighting Design](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/jaipur-walled-city-lighting-design-sarvdeep-basur-lucent-worldwide).
- **Municipal evidence of implementation.** Jaipur Municipal Corporation Heritage’s draft Special Area Heritage Development Plan lists LED illumination of the main bazaar markets as an executed 2021 project. It also documents conservation of the historic urban fabric. This is stronger evidence for permanent infrastructure than a holiday photo. [SAHDP 2041 draft](https://jaipurmcheritage.org/PDF/ImpNotices/SAHDP2041%20-%20Draft.pdf).
- **A dated launch record.** Rajasthan Tourism’s 13 August 2018 tender identifies the inauguration of the Walled City Illumination Project at Choti Chaupar on 15 August 2018. This event document establishes the project, but its event arrangements should not be mistaken for the routine lighting installation. [Rajasthan Tourism inauguration document](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/rajasthan-tourism/english/pdf/tender-pdf/biddoc-13082018.pdf).
- **A direct architectural-lighting precedent.** City Palace’s lighting supplier describes several layers of warm light bringing out detail and depth. It is a separate Jaipur project, useful for visual direction rather than proof of identical equipment in the bazaars. [ILDL: City Palace](https://www.ildlimited.com/portfolio/city-palace/).
- **Nahargarh.** Rajasthan Tourism explicitly describes the fort as floodlit at night and places it on the Aravalli ridge overlooking Jaipur. This supports a softly illuminated fort above a darker hillside. [Rajasthan Tourism: Nahargarh Fort](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/nahargarh-fort.html).

A KTH-hosted architecture thesis records a 2022 interview with designer Sarvdeep Singh Basur. It describes warm lighting between 2400 and 3300 K, stronger warmth in jalis and different tones on columns, and illumination directed at arches and cornices to express depth. Treat these values as interview-reported design intent, not independently measured current conditions. The useful lesson is a family of warm tones, not one flat yellow colour. [Architecture thesis, Jaipur case study, printed page 5](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1683203/FULLTEXT01.pdf).

## Visual references and what to take from them

1. **User’s Hawa Mahal night photograph** — the primary mood reference. The image has alternating illuminated projections and dark setbacks. Bright golden edges retain their architecture because adjacent areas are significantly darker. The sky is violet-black, not pure black. Small coloured panes exist but are accents, not the dominant treatment.
2. **User’s hillfort night photograph** — useful for warm stone against a very dark ridge and sky, layered terraces, and pools of light separated by darkness. Its exact monument identity is unverified; it resembles views often labelled Amer. Do not label it a verified Nahargarh photograph in the finished site or research captions.
3. **Official Hawa Mahal reference** — useful for the relation between pink masonry, jharokhas and pale ornament. [Rajasthan Tourism Hawa Mahal page](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/hawa-mahal.html).
4. **Ordinary street / trial lighting** — arches and cornices stand out while people, scooters and shops continue underneath. This is a living market, not a stage set. [Chaura Rasta lighting trial photograph and report](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/jaipur/in-walled-city-nights-get-a-makeover/articleshow/61679324.cms).
5. **Designer’s project gallery** — project-owned references offer a better indication of intended architectural emphasis than generic travel aggregators. [Lucent Worldwide’s Jaipur gallery](https://www.tfod.in/photo-space/19215/Jaipur-Walled-City-lighting-design-Sarvdeep-Basur-LucentWorldwide).
6. **Blue-hour photographic reference** — useful for warm facade versus lingering blue sky; the photographer describes photographing just after the lights came on. [Photographer’s Hawa Mahal post](https://www.reddit.com/r/photographs/comments/cv9whd). This is a visual reference, not a technical specification.

Online reference photographs are for research only. Do not embed, redistribute or ship third-party photography as site assets; create the light treatment in the project’s own SVG.

## Avoiding the wrong Jaipur mood

Searches for the named bazaars disproportionately return Diwali photographs. Festive bulb canopies, strings spanning roads, star lanterns, temporary stages and rainbow projection should not become the baseline night theme. The official Hawa Mahal Festival tender explicitly includes coloured decorative lights and event fixtures, so it provides a useful exclusion example. [2023 festival tender](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/rajasthan-tourism/english/pdf/tender-pdf/Tender_Hawamahal_Festival_10012023Final.pdf).

Also reject image-search false positives: Kishanpole Gate in **Banswara**, Udaipur’s Surajpole, and generic images republished under different city names. A colourful Tripolia image does not prove all Jaipur streets use the same lighting every night. The requested illustration deliberately favours the warm amber aspect of Jaipur rather than reproducing every possible lighting programme.

## Proposed visual system — original design recommendations

### Day

Keep the existing cream-paper sky, muted pink walls and terracotta lettering. Lights are off. Preserve the user-approved scene, product photography, brand logos, enlarged storefront, worker scale and full delivery journey.

### Evening

Use a dusty rose horizon graduating into subdued plum. Architecture stays recognisably pink; shadows gain cool mauve rather than just becoming black. Begin warm facade illumination at lower strength. Light beneath the Bansal awning should appear before broad monument glow. An amber sun can remain, with restrained halo. The state must look deliberately different from both the original day and full night.

### Night — principal composition

- **Sky:** deep ink-indigo with a small warm-white moon. Sparse quiet stars, if used, remain subordinate. No dense glitter or moving star field.
- **Hawa Mahal:** pink-brown base, honey/amber facade planes, pale golden cornices, dark window interiors. Discrete broad uplight gradients should follow the bays. Architectural lines remain crisp; use blur for light spill only.
- **Hillfort and Parcota wall:** low-intensity gold on visible faces, darker mountain mass. Selected wall segments can catch light, but do not outline every battlement at equal brightness.
- **Bansal shop:** strongest local warmth and clearest ivory lettering. A gentle wash above the sign, glow beneath the awning and a modest pool on the pavement make it the place of welcome. Keep the 1.1× store scale and existing brand panel.
- **Other shops:** smaller amber interior pools, quieter signs. They establish a real bazaar without competing with Bansal.
- **Paving:** cool muted brown/indigo, preserving perspective lines. Add soft elliptical reflected light near lit storefronts; avoid mirror reflections on an otherwise dry street.
- **People and rickshaw:** keep clothing and body detail readable, with a restrained warm edge/spill. Preserve motion geometry and chronology. Lighting should stay attached to the illustrated architecture, not slide with the vehicle.
- **Trees:** deep desaturated green, faint warm edges nearest the shop. Avoid making foliage glow uniformly.

Suggested starting palette, to be judged in-browser: night sky `#14172a`, distant sky `#262138`, unlit masonry `#68473f`, warm facade `#b77949`, cornice light `#f4c77e`, glow core `#ffe6ab`, moon `#fff1cc`. Evening: sky `#66516b`, horizon `#bb7f69`, masonry `#bd806b`. These are design choices, not sampled scientific measurements.

### Website beyond the hero

Carry the ink/plum background, warm ivory text and copper borders through navigation, story, categories, catalogue and footer. Keep products and manufacturer logos on neutral light surfaces so packaging colours remain faithful. Product carousel focus ratios remain 60/82/100/82/60 with the existing subtle peripheral de-emphasis. Warm lighting belongs to the city illustration; do not make every UI panel glow.

## Interaction and acceptance criteria

- Automatic schedule uses **Asia/Kolkata**: day 05:00–17:00, evening 17:00–18:00, night 18:00–05:00. These are the user’s chosen editorial boundaries, not claims about astronomical sunset or real monument-lighting times.
- Sun/moon is an understandable, keyboard-operable control with explicit Day, Evening, Night and Auto choices. Manual choice persists; Auto returns to the Jaipur clock.
- Set the initial theme before first paint; update the automatic state after time boundaries, tab visibility changes and page restoration.
- No flicker, strobing, infinite glow pulses or new motion competing with the delivery journey. Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Inspect desktop and mobile at all three states. Check readable navigation, buttons, story content, full catalogue dialog and packaging; inspect clipping of the moon/control and the enlarged storefront.
- Preserve all six synchronized hero animation tracks and full 14-second delivery sequence. Theme changes must not accidentally restart or truncate it.
- Deliver locally for review; do not publish or push this theme task.

## Research limitations

The material confirms a permanent illumination programme and a strong warm architectural-lighting language. It does not establish one uniform current-night appearance for every named road, exact current on/off hours, or present fixture health. Some direct image/PDF endpoints timed out or blocked fetching; search-index material was used cautiously and triangulated with official records and the supplied images. The final scene should be presented as Bansal’s illustrated Jaipur after sundown, not a survey of the city’s electrical infrastructure.
